# Video hooks — the triple hook method

A hook has one job: make the viewer decide, in the first 1–3 seconds, that the rest of the video is for them and that they need to hear what comes next. The triple hook does that in three moves, in this order.

## 1 · Context

Viewers only watch content where they have a vested interest in the topic. The **first line** must make the topic of the video obvious.

- Say it in the audio, show it in the visual, write it in the on-screen text. Ideally all three, from frame 1.
- The viewer must be able to answer "what is this video about?" before the second sentence starts.
- Common failure: opening with a greeting, a name, a mood, or a vague teaser ("Let me tell you a story…"). None of these establish context.

## 2 · The lean

Make the viewer curious in a specific direction, so they lean into the context and actively want more. Use one of:

- **Proof or a stat** that validates the claim (a number, a company, a result).
- **A specific pain point** the viewer has.
- **A specific benefit** the viewer wants.
- **Something interesting, shocking, or unexpected.**

Specific beats general: "three of the biggest companies in the world" leans less than "Apple, Volvo and Tesla".

**Gate after sentences 1–2:**
1. Is the context immediately clear?
2. Is there a curiosity inducement that makes the viewer lean in?

If either answer is no, rewrite before adding step 3.

## 3 · The contrarian snapback

Hook the fish, let it lean one way, then yank the rod the other way. Once the viewer has formed an expectation from steps 1–2, reverse it.

Template:
> "[context + lean]. But that's the least [impressive / interesting / important] part. The most [impressive / interesting / important] part is [the thing they didn't expect]."

Rules:
- The snapback must reverse a real expectation created by the lean. A soft line like "but it wasn't always easy" is not a snapback: everyone expects it, so nothing is yanked.
- The new direction must itself be a lean: it opens a question the video then answers.
- **The video must pay off the snapback.** If the hook says "the interesting part is X", the body must deliver X. A snapback with no payoff is clickbait and burns trust.

## Output format

When writing a hook, return:

1. **The hook**: spoken text, with each of the three moves labelled.
2. **On-screen text** for frame 1 (the context) and for the snapback moment.
3. **The three checks**, each answered in one line: context clear? lean present? snapback reverses a real expectation, and where in the body is it paid off?
4. **Two alternatives** that use a different lean type (stat / pain / benefit / unexpected).

When auditing an existing hook, score it against the three checks first, name the failing move, then rewrite only what fails.

## House rules for Henrique's series (Os Episódios de Munique)

- Language: European Portuguese (PT-PT) unless asked otherwise. Spoken register, short sentences.
- **If a line could be said by anyone else, it is wrong.** Use his facts: Apple, Volvo, Tesla, Porto, four countries, 2022 with zero internships, Munich.
- Value is delivered *inside* the reel, not promised for later.
- Hook length: context + lean in ≤5 s; the full triple hook in ≤10 s.
- Red lines: never name the current Munich employer or show its office, screens, colleagues or internal processes. Past employers may be named, but never their parts, CAD, simulation results or internal numbers. No absolute salary figures. No investment advice.
- Never open with "Olá, chamo-me…". The name comes after the hook.
