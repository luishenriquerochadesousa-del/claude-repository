01 · Lock before you record

Five decisions, made once, never reopened

Each of these is a thing you will otherwise re-litigate every Saturday at 22:00 instead of filming. Decide now, write them down, stop thinking about them.

Language

Portuguese spoken, English burned-in captions

Your differentiation is Portuguese engineer who got out. That story is worth far more to a PT/BR audience than a generic "dev abroad" story is to an English one, and PT/BR is where the questions — and later the paid product — actually come from. Cost: lower ad RPM. Accept it; your revenue lines are brand deals and your own product, not Instagram's payout.

  • Every video ships with burned-in captions. Silent-autoplay is the default viewing mode.
  • Caption text in the post: Portuguese first, one English line at the end.
Account

One account. Not a UGC account and a personal account.

You were going to make this UGC-only. Don't split it. UGC clients buy the person and the proof; a separate faceless UGC page has no proof and starts at zero. The series is the portfolio — brands can watch you shoot, light and edit for 40 weeks before they hire you.

  • Handle: your real name, or name + a one-word anchor. Not a niche word you'll outgrow.
  • Bio states the three tracks and the promise: episodes weekly.
Format

Episodes are numbered and dated on-screen

The number is the format. It creates a back-catalogue people binge, a reason to follow (they want the next one), and a public counter you can't quietly abandon. Every episode opens with EP. 07 · MUNICH · WEEK 7 in the same corner, same font, forever.

Scope

Three tracks, one at a time on the business side

Body, engineering career, and money experiments. The first two run continuously from week 1. The money track opens one experiment at a time — UGC first, then a digital product, then ecommerce. Running three at once is the most common way this ends.

The one rule that matters: a missed Sunday ends the series; a bad episode does not. In eighteen months nobody will remember which episode was badly lit. Everybody will notice the gap between episode 9 and episode 23.
02 · Content

Four pillars, unequal weight

The episodes are the spine, but the spine is not what recruits an audience. Discovery comes from the career pillar — that is the content strangers search for and send to friends. The episodes convert those strangers into people who come back.

The editing rules that fix your actual problem

You said it yourself: you don't post because you think it has to be edited beautifully. That is not a taste problem, it is an absence of constraints. Constraints below.

  1. 60-minute timer, started before the first clip is imported. When it rings, you export what exists. Not "almost done" — export.
  2. Two takes per line, maximum. A third take means you keep take two. Perfect delivery reads as an ad; a stumble reads as a person.
  3. One template, forever. One caption style, one font, one transition (a hard cut), one colour preset. Choose them in week 1 and never open the font menu again.
  4. Three minutes maximum choosing music, or no music. Spoken-word reels do fine dry.
  5. Nothing gets deleted before it's posted. You are not allowed to judge a video you have not published. Your taste is calibrated for consuming, not for making — it will be wrong for the first six months.
  6. Escape hatch: if 19:00 arrives and the edit is broken, post the single best raw take with a title card. The audience has never once punished this. Missing the week is the only real failure.
  7. Weeks 1–8: never look at the numbers. Turn off the notification. Check reach once, on the Sunday of week 8. Before that the data is noise and it will only make you quit.

06 · Track A

The body — twelve months, four numbers

You are skinny and healthy and want mass. That is the easiest transformation to film and the slowest to feel. The content protocol matters as much as the training: identical framing every month is what makes the change visible, and visible change is what makes people stay.

Record the start line on day one

  • Bodyweight, first thing, empty stomach — and the weekly average, never a single day
  • Waist at the navel, and one relaxed upper-arm measurement
  • Max strict pull-ups, max dips, max push-ups — counted on camera
  • Photos: front, side, back. Same shorts, same spot, same light, marks taped on the floor

Do all of it before episode 2 is shot. Without a start line there is no series, only footage.

Twelve-month targets

  • +7 to 9 kg bodyweight, at no more than ~0.3 kg/week — faster is mostly fat and it films badly
  • 15 strict pull-ups, 20 dips
  • One muscle-up — the single most watchable milestone available to you
  • Waist up no more than ~4 cm over the same period

Training — four sessions, calisthenics

  • Pull: weighted pull-ups, ring rows, curls with the rings or a band
  • Push: weighted dips, ring push-ups, pike push-ups
  • Legs: Bulgarian split squats, nordic curls, calf raises — do not skip these; skipped legs are visible in every wide shot
  • Skill + core: muscle-up progression, front-lever work, hanging leg raises

Progressive overload needs load: a dip belt and a weighted vest are the two purchases that make bodyweight training hypertrophic. Rings over a fixed bar wherever possible.

Eating — the part that will actually decide it

  • Roughly +300 kcal over maintenance, adjusted monthly from the weight trend, not the mirror
  • ~1.8 g protein per kg of bodyweight — for you that is the whole problem, not training volume
  • Three fixed meals plus two calorie-dense snacks. Skinny people under-eat by accident, not by choice
  • Film the food occasionally. It is the most-asked question you will get, and it is pillar C content for free
Filming protocol: first Sunday of every month, same corner, same shorts, same time of day, floor marks taped down. One training clip a week for pillar C. Numbers on screen in every single one — the counter going up is the retention device, not the physique.
07 · Track B

The money — one experiment at a time, in this order

Sequenced by capital required and by how much audience each one needs. Running them in parallel is the mistake that kills this; each one gets an open date, a budget cap and a kill date.

01

UGC — paid content for brands

Opens week 9 · capital ≈ €0 · kill date: 60 days after first pitch

The fastest cash line and the one you were already planning. You are not selling reach, you are selling production: a brand pays for the video, then runs it as their own ad. Build a three-video portfolio using products you actually own, then pitch ten brands a week — small DTC brands, not the ones with agencies. Price at €150–350 per video to start; raise it after five paid jobs.

03

Ecommerce / dropshipping

Opens only after experiment 1 or 2 produces revenue · hard capital cap

Last, not first. It is the only one of the three that can lose money, it is the most operationally heavy while you are settling into a new job, and in the EU it now carries real product-compliance obligations (see below). Set a capital cap you can lose without flinching — €800 is a reasonable ceiling — and a written kill date. Whatever the ad account says on that date decides it, not your feelings about it. 08 · Instrumentation

Five numbers weekly, and the gates they open

Track these

  • % reach from non-followers — the only real growth signal
  • 3-second retention and average watch time — tells you if the hook works
  • Saves + shares per post — the strongest distribution signal there is
  • Follower delta, weekly, never daily
  • Questions logged from comments and DMs — your content backlog

Ignore these

  • Likes
  • Total view count on any single post
  • Follower count between Sundays
  • Any comparison to an account older than yours
  • Everything, entirely, for the first eight weeks

Review rhythm

  • Weekly: nothing. Post and close the app.
  • Monthly: the five numbers into a sheet, 15 minutes.
  • Quarterly: the on-camera review. Kill what failed. This is an episode.

09 · Constraints

What being an employed engineer in Germany actually restricts

This is the section most creator plans skip and most creators get bitten by. Handle it in the first month, while you are earning nothing — it is trivial then and expensive later.

Do first

Your employment contract, before you earn a cent

German employment contracts routinely require you to notify — or get written approval for — any Nebentätigkeit (secondary activity). Read your new contract's clause, and if approval is needed, ask for it in writing before the first invoice. Framed as content creation and a small side business with no competitive overlap, this is almost always granted. Doing it after the fact is what turns it into a problem.

Hard line

Never film the job

No office, no screens, no badge, no colleagues, no product detail, no roadmap, no internal process, no interview questions you were asked as an interviewer. Consider not naming the employer at all — “one of the biggest companies in the world” is a stronger line anyway and carries none of the risk. Talk about your career, publicly known facts, and your own preparation. That is where all of the value is, and none of the exposure.

Month 1

Registration and tax

  • Monetised creator work is normally a trade: Gewerbeanmeldung at the local Gewerbeamt (small one-off fee), then the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung with the Finanzamt
  • Small-business VAT relief (Kleinunternehmerregelung, §19 UStG) keeps you out of VAT below the turnover thresholds — confirm the current figures, they were raised recently
  • Income declared annually alongside your employment income
  • Keep every invoice and receipt from day one, including equipment
Ongoing

Disclosure and Impressum

  • Any paid or gifted collaboration must be labelled Werbung / Anzeige, clearly and up front — not buried in hashtags. German courts are strict and competitors send warning letters over this
  • A commercially operated account generally needs a reachable Impressum — a link in bio to a page with your legal details
  • Affiliate links get labelled too
Before the store

Ecommerce into the EU is not what YouTube says

  • Consumers get a 14-day right of withdrawal (Widerrufsrecht) with mandatory instructions
  • EU product-safety rules require an identifiable responsible person inside the EU for most goods — which breaks the classic ship-direct-from-Asia model
  • Imports carry customs and import VAT; delivery times from Asian suppliers are a returns problem, not a detail

None of this makes it impossible. It makes the naive version impossible, which is exactly why it belongs last in the sequence and behind a capital cap.

Reference

Rules are described here, not given

Thresholds, fees and product rules change, and your contract is specific to you. Spend one hour with a Steuerberater before the first invoice — it costs less than one UGC job and it removes the single largest reason people stall before monetising.

11 · Risk

The seven ways this ends early

1 · Editing perfectionism

Your stated problem. Solved only by the 60-minute timer and the raw-take escape hatch — never by getting better at editing.

2 · Quitting in week 6

The numbers will be flat and you will conclude it doesn't work. It doesn't work yet. That is why analytics stay off until week 8.

3 · Three experiments at once

UGC and a store and a product in the same month means three half-built things and no episodes. One at a time, gated.

4 · Talking instead of doing

Episodes about plans are not a series. Every episode carries proof of something done that week, even if the thing done was small or failed.

5 · Only posting episodes

The documentary spine does not recruit strangers. Pillar B does. Drop it and growth stops.

6 · Employer or legal exposure

One filmed screen, one unlabelled paid post. Handled in month one, this risk is zero.

7 · The relocation eats month one

Moving cities will destroy at least one week's shoot. Batch two extra episodes before you pack — pre-recorded pillar B content has no expiry date and is exactly what a buffer is for.
