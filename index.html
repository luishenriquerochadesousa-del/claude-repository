Portugal · updated August 2026

You don't need to own the flat. You do need to own the compliance.

Running short-term rentals in someone else's property is entirely legal in Portugal — provided the owner's authorisation is in writing and the registration is in the right name. What kills these businesses is rarely the model: it's a missing signature from a landlord, a freguesia that stopped issuing registrations, or a rent that eats 45% of gross revenue. This dashboard covers all three.

The honest recommendation

Start co-hosting, then graduate to arbitrage on units you've already proven. Portuguese rents relative to achievable ADR make pure arbitrage thin — a Lisbon T1 at €1,100/month needs roughly €2,800–3,300 of gross bookings just to earn a normal margin, which is a high bar outside prime months. Co-hosting pays 15–25% of gross with none of the rent risk, gets you the operational reps, and — this is the real asset — puts you in front of owners whose flats you may later want to lease outright. Every serious operator I can find in Portugal built the book this way.

02 — Enquadramento legal

The Alojamento Local regime, current state

Everything short-term-rental in Portugal runs off one statute: Decreto-Lei n.º 128/2014, de 29 de agosto, the regime jurídico da exploração dos estabelecimentos de alojamento local. It has been amended six times. What matters is which version you're reading — most blog posts online are still describing the 2023 rules, which were repealed.

How the law got here

  1. 1
    DL 128/2014 — the base regime

    Creates AL as a category distinct from hotels (DL 80/2017 governs those), defines the modalities and the comunicação prévia registration route. This distinction matters later for VAT.

  2. 2
    Lei 62/2018 — municipalities get teeth

    Introduces áreas de contenção, the mandatory civil liability insurance, and per-building limits on apartment-modality registrations.

  3. 3
    Lei 56/2023 "Mais Habitação" — the freeze

    Suspended new AL registrations in apartments nationwide, imposed the CEAL extraordinary contribution, and set licences to expire in 2030. Almost all of this is now repealed — ignore any guide that still describes it.

  4. 4
    DL 76/2024 — the reversal (in force 1 Nov 2024)

    Lifted the national freeze, killed the CEAL, removed the 2030 expiry, and made registrations transferable with the property instead of personal and non-transmissible. It also removed the requirement for prior condominium authorisation — while giving condominiums a new, easier opposition right.

    Sixth amendmentMost restrictions lifted
  5. 5
    DL 151/2026 — the municipal deadline

    Exceptional regime: municipalities with more than 1,000 registered AL units as at 31 Dec 2025 have until 31 December 2026 to write (or rewrite) their local regulation, and may keep suspending new registrations meanwhile. Any such suspension lapses automatically at the end of 2026 if no regulation is in force. This is the single biggest live variable for anyone starting now.

    Deadline 31 Dec 2026Lisboa · Porto · Funchal · Sintra affected
  6. 6
    EU Regulation 2024/1028 — fully applicable since 20 May 2026

    Platforms (Airbnb, Booking, Expedia) must now systematically verify registration numbers against national registries and share host data with authorities. Practical consequence: an unregistered or mismatched listing gets pulled automatically. The era of quietly listing without an RNAL number is over.

Requirements the unit must meet

Physical & safety

  • Adequate conservation, hygiene and functioning of all equipment
  • Natural ventilation — windows or balcony doors to the outside
  • Bathroom with running hot and cold water
  • Fire extinguisher and fire blanket, accessible to guests
  • First-aid kit
  • National emergency number (112) displayed visibly
  • Manual or automatic fire-safety measures scale with capacity (units above 10 guests attract heavier requirements)

Paperwork that must exist before guest one

  • Civil liability insurance — mandatory, minimum cover €75,000 per claim, covering fire and damage to guests and third parties. Proof is uploaded to the registration.
  • Placa identificativa — the standardised AL plaque, fixed to the outside of the building, next to the entrance
  • Livro de reclamações eletrónico — registration on the national platform is mandatory
  • RNAL number displayed in every listing and advertisement
Contraordenações — what non-compliance costs

Operating, advertising or intermediating an unregistered AL establishment: €2,500–€4,000 for an individual, €25,000–€40,000 for a company. Missing plaque, missing insurance, missing complaints book and out-of-date registration data all carry their own fines. Enforcement is ASAE, plus the município, and since May 2026 the platforms themselves flag mismatches. Repeat offences can bring closure of the establishment.

03 — Registration

Getting the RNAL number when the flat isn't yours

Registration is a comunicação prévia com prazo — a prior notice, not a licence application. You file, you get a number immediately, and the município has a window in which to inspect and object. The one non-obvious part for a non-owner is proving legitimidade.

  1. 1
    Open activity at Finanças first

    You need an active CAE before you can register. The usual codes are CAE 55201 (alojamento mobilado para turistas) and CAE 55204 (outros locais de alojamento de curta duração). Do it on the Portal das Finanças under Início de Atividade, as a trabalhador independente or through a company. Register with Segurança Social at the same time.

    FreeSame day, online
  2. 2
    Assemble the legitimacy file — the part that trips up non-owners

    The comunicação prévia requires the title under which you use the property. As a tenant that means: the lease contract plus the owner's express written authorisation to operate Alojamento Local in the property and to sublet. Under art. 1038.º f) and art. 1088.º do Código Civil a tenant may not give a third party the use of the property, and subletting authorisation must be in writing. Verbal comfort is worthless: unauthorised subletting is grounds for terminating your lease, which would take the AL registration with it.

    Deal-breaker if missing
  3. 3
    File at the Balcão Único Eletrónico

    Via eportugal.gov.pt / the Balcão do Empreendedor, addressed to the president of the câmara municipal. Typical attachments: ID and NIF of the titular da exploração, caderneta predial urbana, the use title (lease + authorisation), a floor plan with rooms identified, declaration of compliance with the legal requirements, and the insurance certificate.

  4. 4
    Get the number, put up the plaque, start operating

    The RNAL number is issued automatically on submission and is the only document you need in order to operate (art. 7.º, as amended by DL 76/2024). You may begin immediately unless the município objects.

  5. 5
    Survive the inspection window

    The município inspects within 60 days of the prior notice, or 90 days if the unit sits in an área de contenção, to verify the requirements. Keep the safety kit, plaque and insurance in place from day one — an inspection failing on a €20 fire blanket is an avoidable way to lose the registration.

  6. 6
    Keep the registration current

    Any change to the registered data must be communicated within 10 days. Insurance renewals get uploaded. Cessation of activity must be communicated too. Since DL 76/2024 the registration is no longer personal — it travels with the property on transfer — which cuts both ways for a tenant-operator: a new owner inherits a live registration, but also inherits the power to end your lease.

Condominium: authorisation is out, opposition is in

DL 76/2024 removed the need for prior condominium authorisation where the fraction is designated for housing in the título constitutivo. In exchange, the condominium can now oppose the activity by a deliberation carrying more than 50% of the building's permilagem — down from the previous two-thirds — where there is repeated conduct disturbing normal use of the building. Separately, if the título constitutivo expressly prohibits non-residential use, AL is off the table regardless. Read the título constitutivo and the last two years of assembly minutes before you sign a lease.

The strategic read

Contention pushes new operators outward, and that's not purely bad news. A registration in a closed zone is now a scarce asset — existing AL units transfer with the property and command a premium, and a landlord who already holds a live RNAL registration in Alfama is exactly the counterparty you want for a cessão de exploração rather than a fresh registration. Meanwhile Setúbal, Almada, Braga, Coimbra, Aveiro and the second-line Algarve towns still register freely and have far better rent-to-ADR ratios than Lisbon centre.

05 — Contracts

Four ways to paper the deal with the owner

This is where the business is won or lost. Pick the structure deliberately — they differ in who is liable, who registers, and what VAT the owner has to charge you.

A · Lease + written AL authorisation

A standard arrendamento with an express clause authorising subletting and AL exploitation. You register the AL in your name. Cleanest for arbitrage. Requires art. 1088.º CC written authorisation; the sublease must then be communicated to the landlord.

Best for controlYou carry rent risk

B · Cessão de exploração

The owner transfers operation of a going concern (the AL establishment, furnished and equipped) to you for a fee. Not a lease, so the tenancy protections of the NRAU don't apply the same way. Common in professional management. Note the tax consequence below.

FlexibleOwner may owe 23% VAT on your payment

C · Management mandate (co-hosting)

You act in the owner's name for a commission on gross bookings, typically 15–25%. The owner registers, insures, invoices the guests and carries the tax. You invoice a service fee. Fastest to start, zero rent risk.

Zero capitalLower ceiling per unit

D · Revenue-share hybrid

Guaranteed floor to the owner plus a split above it (e.g. €800 guaranteed + 40% of gross above €2,000). Bridges the gap between an owner who wants certainty and an operator who can't absorb a full fixed rent. Increasingly the standard offer.

Easiest to sellModel it carefully

Watch the VAT on what you pay the owner

An AT binding information from March 2025 (PIV 27356) reversed the previous position: the reduced 6% rate applies to the invoice from the operator to the guest, but the amount the property owner receives from the operator is treated as an atypical service beyond mere accommodation and taxed at the standard 23%. A pure arrendamento is VAT-exempt under art. 9.º, n.º 29 CIVA; a cessão de exploração generally is not. That is a 23% swing on your single largest cost line — settle it with an accountant before you choose structure B over structure A.

07 — Fiscalidade

Tax: the four things that decide your net

AL income is business income — categoria B — not rental income. That single classification drives everything below. Figures are for 2026; thresholds move most years.

1 · IRS — regime simplificado

Under art. 31.º CIRS a coefficient converts turnover into taxable income; the remainder is presumed to be costs.

  • 0.35 — AL in moradia or apartamento: 35% of gross is taxed, 65% presumed costs
  • 0.50 — the same activity in an área de contenção: half your gross becomes taxable
  • 0.15 — hotel-type and similar establishments, including some hospedagem modalities

Taxable income is then added to your other income and taxed at progressive IRS rates. Above €200,000 turnover you must move to contabilidade organizada, where actual costs are deducted instead.

2 · IVA

  • Isenção art. 53.º CIVA while turnover stays under €15,000 (threshold set by DL 35/2025). Exceed it and you leave the exemption; exceeding €18,750 mid-year ends it immediately.
  • Once inside the normal regime, accommodation invoiced to guests goes at the reduced 6% rate (verba 2.17, Lista I) per AT's current position, while ancillary services billed separately follow their own rates.
  • Reverse charge on platform commissions. Airbnb (Ireland) and Booking (Netherlands) invoice you without VAT. You must register in VIES and self-assess Portuguese VAT at 23% on those commissions — even if you are art. 53.º exempt, in which case you cannot deduct it. It is a real cost and the single most commonly missed obligation in this business.

3 · Segurança Social

As a trabalhador independente: 21.4% on relevant income, with quarterly declarations. For AL and other hotel-type activity the relevant income is 20% of turnover (not the 70% that applies to ordinary services) — a large difference.

Two reliefs worth confirming with an accountant: a 12-month exemption from contributions when you first open activity, and a carve-out under which independents whose activity is exclusively urban letting for AL in moradia or apartamento fall outside the contributory regime altogether. Whether the carve-out survives once you're operating multiple units as a business is exactly the question to put to a contabilista certificado.

4 · The rest

  • Retenção na fonte — 25% withholding on B2B service invoices you issue, unless you're exempt in your first year under the €15,000 threshold.
  • Modelo 30 — due when you pay income to non-resident entities.
  • IMI / condominium — the owner's, not yours, and it should say so in the contract.
  • Company vs. sole trader — a sociedade por quotas pays IRC at 20% on the first €50,000 of taxable profit plus derrama, deducts real costs, and ring-fences liability. It becomes the better structure at roughly 3+ units or once you're above the simplified-regime ceiling.
Worked example — one unit, simplified regime

€24,000 gross bookings in the year, non-contention zone. IRS: €24,000 × 0.35 = €8,400 added to taxable income. Segurança Social: €24,000 × 20% × 21.4% ≈ €1,027 (before any exemption). IVA: under €15,000? No — so you're in the normal regime, charging 6% on accommodation and self-assessing 23% on roughly €2,000 of platform commissions. The cash cost of tax and contributions on that unit lands somewhere around €3,000–4,500 depending on your marginal IRS bracket.

    The rule that saves you from bad deals

    Rent should not exceed 28–30% of realistic gross revenue, and realistic means the annual average, not August. Below 25% the deal is good; above 35% you are working for the landlord.     10 — Execution

    The first 90 days

    1. 1
      Days 1–14 · Pick a market you can actually register in

      Call the câmara municipal's atividades económicas desk and ask directly whether the freguesia is in contenção and whether a suspension is running under DL 151/2026. Then pull 20–30 comparable listings on AirDNA or by hand on Airbnb, note ADR and calendar density for the last 6 months, and compute the rent-to-gross ratio for real advertised rents. If nothing clears 30%, change market — don't change your assumptions.

    2. 2
      Days 10–30 · Build the offer, then source landlords

      Landlords say yes to certainty, not to upside. Lead with: guaranteed rent paid on the 1st, professional cleaning every turnover, €75,000 liability insurance, quarterly inspections they're invited to, the property returned furnished and maintained. Source from: long-vacant listings on Idealista/OLX (30+ days is a motivated owner), owners already holding an RNAL registration who are tired of operating it, small local real-estate agencies, and — most productive of all — condominium administrators.

      Expect 1 yes per 25–40 approaches
    3. 3
      Days 20–40 · Paper it properly

      Lawyer reviews the lease, the AL authorisation, the título constitutivo and the last two years of condominium minutes. Budget €300–600. This is the cheapest insurance you will ever buy.

    4. 4
      Days 30–50 · Register and fit out in parallel

      Open activity → file the comunicação prévia → insurance → plaque → safety kit → furniture → photos. Registration is instant, so the fit-out is your critical path.

    5. 5
      Days 45–60 · Launch pricing deliberately underpriced

      New listings have no reviews and no ranking. Open 20–25% below comparable ADR for the first 8–10 bookings, enforce a shorter minimum stay to accumulate reviews faster, then raise. Get to 10 reviews before you optimise for rate.

    6. 6
      Days 60–90 · Systemise, then decide

      Cleaner with a written checklist and photo verification; PMS with a channel manager (Hostaway, Guesty, Smoobu, Lodgify) so Airbnb and Booking share one calendar; dynamic pricing (PriceLabs, Wheelhouse); automated SIBA reporting. Then look at 60 days of real numbers and decide whether door two is the same model or a different one.

    11 — Risk

    What actually goes wrong

    Verbal authorisation

    The owner is enthusiastic, you skip the written clause, the flat sells 14 months later and the new owner terminates. You lose the fit-out and the registration. Written, or don't sign.

    The condominium turns

    Neighbours document noise, an assembly passes an opposition deliberation with 50%+ of permilagem, and your operation stops while the lease continues. Mitigate with strict house rules, noise sensors, no parties, and — genuinely — introducing yourself to the neighbours before the first guest.

    Regulatory whiplash

    Portugal froze new registrations in 2023 and unfroze them in 2024; municipalities are rewriting rules through December 2026. Never build a plan that only works under the current rules. Keep the exit clause.

    Seasonality kills cash, not profit

    An annually profitable unit can still run out of money in February. Hold three months of fixed costs per door, and consider medium-term (30+ day) lets in the low season — often better than an empty calendar and outside much of the AL friction.

    Silent tax liabilities

    The 23% reverse charge on platform commissions and the VAT exemption threshold catch people 18 months in, with interest. Get a contabilista certificado from month one — €50–90/month.

    Over-leveraging on door one

    Signing three leases before the first has run a full low season is the most common way these businesses die. Prove one through a winter first.
